# Virtual Experience: The Rain God's Chariot

**The Rain God’s Festival** is an interactive virtual museum inspired by the Nepalese festival Rato Machhindranath Jatra, a cultural procession dedicated to the rain god, whose blessings are believed to bring rainfall essential for agriculture.

 Developed at **Birmingham Open Media**, the project recreates the atmosphere, architecture, movement, and spiritual essence of the festival through a fully explorable 3D environment. Visitors walk through a digitally reconstructed town, witness the iconic chariot procession, and conclude their journey at the sacred Swayambhunath Stupa, accompanied by peaceful Buddhist chants.

## Experience Flow
- Visitors begin by navigating through replicated local architecture, surrounded by ambient sounds of the town.

- At the center of the environment stands a five-storey chariot, animated and accompanied by traditional musical instruments and festival sounds.

- Local residents pull the chariot, recreating the energy and movement of the real procession.

- Visitors can climb nearby buildings to observe the festival from elevated viewpoints, mirroring how locals engage during the celebration.

- Directional glowing orbs guide the visitor along the intended path.

- The journey concludes after ascending a long staircase up a hill to Swayambhunath Stupa, where calm Buddhist mantra music signals the end of the experience.

## User Feedback & Observations
During the project showcase, several usability insights emerged:
 * Overall positive feedback for a unique experience 
 * Some visitors found keyboard controls unintuitive, and occasionally uncertain about where to go
 * Feedback highlighted the need for a clearer in-world guide, potentionally:
   * A MetaHuman acting as a local friend
   * A festival participant guiding visitors through the experience
   * A narrative companion rather than a static guide
  
## Future Improvements
* VR implementation for more intuitive navigation
* A dynamic MetaHuman guide who walks alongside the visitor
* Enhanced visual cues for wayfinding
* Expanded conversational depth for cultural storytelling
